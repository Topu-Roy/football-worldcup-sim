# Content Guide

Good copy is the product. If a page reads like filler, it fails, even when the SEO is right.

This guide applies to every page, post, label and error message.

## Voice

Write like a friendly person who knows football and wants to help.

- Warm, calm, direct.
- Confident without hype.
- Curious about the game, not about keywords.
- Honest about limits. Say "we think" when it is an opinion.

The reader should feel they are talking to a fan who built something useful, not to a marketing team.

## Sentences

- Keep most sentences under 20 words.
- One idea per sentence.
- Use the active voice.
- Start with the point.
- Break long thoughts into two sentences.
- Read it aloud. If you run out of breath, cut it.

## Words

Use words people say out loud.

- Prefer "use" over "utilise".
- Prefer "help" over "facilitate".
- Prefer "build" over "construct".
- Prefer "pick" over "select" when it fits.
- Explain football terms the first time. Not every reader knows "pivot" or "false nine".

Avoid these, they sound like a robot wrote them:

- dive into, unlock, elevate, seamless, game-changer, cutting-edge
- in today's fast-paced world, look no further, whether you're a fan or...
- revolutionary, world-class, ultimate guide
- "It's important to note that", "When it comes to", "In order to"
- Three adjectives in a row. One is enough.

## Robotic patterns to cut

These patterns make copy sound generated. Cut them on sight.

- **Not X but Y.** "A match engine, not a coin flip." State what it is: "Every match is simulated."
- **One-line closers.** A short paragraph that restates the point: "That is the real win." Delete it.
- **Aphorisms.** "The heart of the game", "the real question is", "at its core". Replace with the specific claim.
- **Staged openers.** "Heads up", "Let's dive in", "Here's the thing". Start with the point.
- **Forced triads.** Three parallel examples that only exist for rhythm. Merge them or develop one.
- **Em dashes in prose.** Use a period, comma, colon or parentheses. Title separators are fine.
- **Inflated significance.** "Pivotal moment", "stands as a testament", "marks a turning point". Keep the fact, drop the ceremony.
- **Sales language.** "Unlock", "seamless", "elevate", "world-class", "take it to the next level".
- **Stacked fragments.** "No account. No download. Just play." One short sentence is fine. Five in a row is not.
- **Chatbot residue.** "I hope this helps", "Let me know", "Great question".

## What makes copy valuable

A page earns its place when it gives the reader something they cannot get by skimming Wikipedia.

Include at least three of these:

- A specific example: a match, a player, a season, a scoreline.
- A number with context: ratings, goals, win rates, minutes.
- A tradeoff or counterpoint: what this team was bad at, what the pick costs you.
- A clear explanation of how something works.
- A decision the reader can make after reading.
- A detail from building or testing the game.

Depth comes from specifics, not from length. 1,000 useful words beat 2,000 vague ones. Aim for 1000-1200 words on content pages.

## Structure

Open with the answer or the tension. Do not warm up.

- First sentence: what this is and why it matters.
- Then the detail, in a logical order.
- Short paragraphs. Two to four sentences.
- Subheads should say something. "What made Brazil 1970 different" beats "Overview".
- End with a clear next step, not a summary.

## Page patterns

### Product page

- What it is, in one sentence.
- How it works, step by step.
- What makes it different. Be concrete.
- Who it is for, and who it is not for.
- What it does not do.
- Start playing.

### How-to

- What you will end up with.
- Steps in order, one action each.
- A tip after the hard step, not before it.
- Common mistakes.
- What to try next.

### Explainer or debate

- State the question plainly.
- Give the short answer early.
- Show the evidence.
- Show the counterargument.
- Say what the simulator changes, if anything.
- Let the reader decide.

### Comparison

- Name the options.
- Compare on the things that matter: freedom, depth, speed, cost, friction.
- Be fair to competitors. Readers can tell when you are not.
- Recommend one for a specific kind of player.

### Team or XI page

- Who they were, in two sentences.
- The shape and why it worked.
- Three players who defined it.
- The weakness.
- A moment that sums them up.
- Try the team in the game.

## SEO without sounding like SEO

- The title and H1 can be clear and plain. No keyword stuffing.
- Use the reader's words naturally in the first paragraph.
- Answer the main question in the first 100 words.
- Put FAQ answers in real sentences. No 40-word fragments.
- Internal links should help the reader continue, not feed a crawler.
- Never write a paragraph just to include a phrase.

## Localization

- Translate meaning, not words.
- Keep the same warmth and the same reading level.
- Do not machine-translate and publish. A human must read it.
- Football terms differ by country. Use the local term. For example, "Elfmeterschießen", "tirs au but", "penales", "PK戦".
- Keep names, ratings and dates identical across languages.
- Keep the section structure aligned with English. If a locale needs an extra sentence, add it because the language needs it, not because the draft grew.
- Use the local word for football. Do not leave "soccer", "match day" or "star power" in German, French or Spanish.
- Check for false friends and literal phrases. "Deep squad" is not "un effectif profond" in every context; write what a local fan would say.

## Quality checklist

Before publishing, answer yes to all:

1. Does the first paragraph tell the reader something useful?
2. Are there at least three specific details?
3. Is there one idea the reader can repeat to a friend?
4. Did I cut every sentence that only fills space?
5. Does it sound like a person speaking?
6. Would I read this if I did not work here?
7. Does it follow [seo-strategy.md](./seo-strategy.md)?

If any answer is no, rewrite it. Do not publish and hope.

## Examples

Bad:

> In today's fast-paced world of football gaming, My World Cup XI offers a seamless and revolutionary way to dive into the ultimate fantasy experience.

Good:

> Pick a formation. Draft eleven players from World Cup history. Then watch your team play a full tournament. It takes about five minutes to start.

Bad:

> Brazil 1970 are widely regarded as one of the greatest teams of all time, boasting a wealth of legendary talent across every position.

Good:

> Brazil 1970 won all six matches and scored 19 goals. Five players could run the game. That is why so many people still call them the best.

Bad:

> Our advanced simulation engine utilises sophisticated algorithms to deliver unparalleled realism.

Good:

> The engine plays each match in fifteen-minute chunks. Possession, stamina and chemistry change the chances as the game goes on.

## Final rule

If a sentence could appear on any football site, it is not good enough.

Make it specific to this game, this team, or this reader.
